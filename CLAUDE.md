# Snow Dream (Pegasus)

Godot 4.7 .NET (C# only, no GDScript), desktop. Hobby project: keep it simple (YAGNI, KISS), clean modules, no backward compatibility.

## Layout

Folders follow the architecture layers. A layer only uses layers below it.

| Layer | Folders |
|---|---|
| Scenes | `day/`, `night/`, `puzzles/`, `ui/` |
| Shared gameplay | `world/` (player, camera, interactables), `conditions/`, `dialogue/` |
| Core services (autoloads) | `core/` (GameState, SceneRouter, SaveService, AudioService) |
| Content (data, not code) | `content/` (Resource `.tres` files, `.yarn` scripts) |

Flow: `core/Boot` enters the world from the state; `core/DayCycle` runs bed → 3 AM night → morning and saves at both; `core/SceneRouter` (autoload) changes scenes behind a fade and pauses the tree meanwhile. `ui/Toast` (autoload) shows one line of feedback.

Dialogue: Yarn Spinner for Godot is vendored in `addons/YarnSpinner-Godot` (MIT, v0.3.23, upstream commit b0abe88); do not edit it. Scripts live in `content/dialogue/*.yarn` (one `dialogue.yarnproject`). `ui/dialogue/conversation.tscn` (autoload `Conversation`) runs them through our own `ui/dialogue/DialogueBox`. Start one with `Conversation.Start("Node")` or an `world/npc/npc.tscn` with a `CharacterProfile` (`content/characters/*.tres`; register it in the Conversation scene's Characters list). Yarn reads and writes game state only through `dialogue/YarnBindings.cs` (`met("...")`, `<<stat>>`, `<<give>>`, `<<flag>>`, `<<bond>>`, `<<wake_up "why">>` to end the night...); never keep game facts in Yarn `$variables`. Choices tagged `#needs:guts>=2` stay visible but locked (write the condition with no spaces, and no `<`, `$` or `#`, e.g. `#needs:guts>=2|item:flashlight`); `<<if>>` on a choice hides it instead. Every choice group needs at least one choice the player can always take: if all are locked or hidden the box shows them and then ends the conversation (the vendored runner cannot fall through). Input ownership: `core/InputLock` (dialogue, fades, later menus and puzzles).

Day places: to add one, make a `LocationData` in `content/locations/*.tres`, a scene whose root script is `day/DayLocation` (or derives from it) with that data, an exit, an instance of `ui/hud/day_hud.tscn` set as its `Hud`, and a `world/bounds/Bounds` node around the walkable area, then add the `.tres` to the `Locations` list in `day/city_map/city_map.tscn`. Every scene with open edges needs a `Bounds` node. Activities: an `ActivityData` in `content/activities/*.tres` (title, verb, gains), offered by a `day/ActivitySpot` in the scene and listed in the place's `LocationData.Activities` for the map. Doing one spends a slot behind a fade (`SceneRouter.Interlude`). A hangout is an activity with `With` (a CharacterProfile), `Bond = 1` and a `YarnScene` prefix: it plays `{YarnScene}1` to `3` by the bond level it raises, then `{YarnScene}Again`; the activity adds the bond afterwards, so hangout scripts don't `<<bond>>`. Place the person as `world/npc/npc.tscn` with no `YarnNode` (Talk stays off) and an `ActivitySpot` at the same spot. Stats and bonds cap at 3 (`GameState.MaxStat`, `MaxBond`).

Items: an `ItemData` in `content/items/{id}.tres` (the file name is the id; look it up with `world/items/Items.Get(id)`). An item with `Warmth > 0` is night gear: `core/state/Warmth` adds it to the base and the Fitness bonus. Give one from Yarn with `<<give id>>`, or sell it: a `day/ShopData` in `content/shops/*.tres` lists the stock, a `day/ShopSpot` in the scene opens it, and the place's `LocationData.Shops` lists it for the map. Browsing is free; buying goes through `GameState.TryBuy`. The Tab menu (`ui/menu`, autoload `Menu`) shows status, items and the notebook; it opens only when the input lock is free and a player is in the scene, and pauses the tree while open.

Night: `night/snow_area` is the one snow area, a grey-box mirror of the neighbourhood. Warmth starts at `Items.MaxWarmth()` and drains at `Warmth.DrainPerSecond` (tune `Warmth.BaseSeconds`) only while `InputLock.IsFree`, so thinking (dialogue, menu, close-ups) is free and walking costs. At zero, or at the home door, `DayCycle.WakeUp` ends the night; nothing is lost but the rest of it. The night HUD (`ui/hud/night_hud.tscn`) shows only Warmth, the bike light (F, once he owns `flashlight`) and the interact prompt. The camera looks north from the south of the player, so keep anything south of a walkable path under about 3 m or it hides him.

Gates: any `Interactable` or `Hotspot` takes `Requires` (a condition) and `LockedYarnNode`, which plays instead until the condition holds. The locked line may hint at the kind of help (more nerve, a real light, someone who reads the old script) but never names items or flags. Most gates take two keys, a stat or a friend's help (`guts >= 2 | item:flashlight`). To block the way, use `world/Gate` (a StaticBody3D with `OpenWhen`): solid and visible while closed, with a child `Examine` for the locked line; once open it hides, stops colliding and turns that off. Night route: lanterns open the hall (stone tablet, Knowledge or Aoi's notes); the underpass (Guts or the flashlight) leads to the arcade and Kissa Mori, the room-escape (`puzzles/cafe`): its stove lock answers the tablet's rule and the shrine's matches light it, so it needs both branches. Sitting down to sleep there ends the night; lighting the stove sets `cafe_fire_lit` for the ending.

Puzzles: each puzzle is its own scene in `puzzles/<name>/` whose root derives from `puzzles/Puzzle` (`Title`, `SolvedFlag`). Each child Control is one view (a wall, named for the title bar), holding `puzzles/Hotspot` buttons (a tag `Label` and a `YarnNode` for what he sees) over placeholder art. The `CloseUp` autoload (`puzzles/close_up/`) frames it as in the mockup: title, arrows between views, his items along the bottom (pick one, then a hotspot, or another item to combine them by `puzzles/rules/Recipes`; the result's line is the Yarn node `Made_{id}`) and Warmth paused. It holds the input lock and closes if the scene changes. Esc calls `Puzzle.StepBack()`, which by default steps back and drops partial progress (a locked room overrides it, as the coffee shop does); `Opened()` can say something on the way in. Steps that last (a lock opened, an item placed) are flags or items; only fiddly state like dials resets. The solved flag is saved, which the 3D world shows with `world/ShowIf` (visual only, no collision). Open a puzzle from the world with a `puzzles/PuzzleSpot`. If it has rules to get wrong (an order, a combination), put them in a pure class under `puzzles/rules/` (linked into `tests/`) and test the solution and a wrong try. Every puzzle's answer must be findable in the night world alone (day clues only help; the way in may still be a gate that takes a stat or a friend, like the tablet's), with no fail penalty. Examine text goes through the dialogue box (`world/interaction/Examine` in 3D, `Hotspot.YarnNode` in close-ups), and reading a clue sets a `clue_*` flag for the notebook.

Notebook: every `<<flag clue_*>>` needs an entry at `content/clues/{flag}.tres` (`ui/notebook/ClueData`: `Title` like "Shrine · Lanterns", `Text`, `Order` to group by place: shrine 10s, underpass 20s, Kissa Mori 30s, and `ByDay` for things heard from friends). An entry records what he saw or heard and stands alone (quote the words he needs); no objectives, next steps or hints. The menu's notebook column lists the entries he has, and the first time a clue flag is set a "Notebook updated" toast follows the conversation.

UI: all styling lives in `ui/theme.tres`, the project's default theme. Give nodes a type variation from it (`NavyPanel`, `CreamPanel`, `TagOrange`, `KeyCap`, `PrimaryButton`, `ChoiceButton`...) rather than colour or stylebox overrides, so the A6 UI kit is a theme swap. Build new screens as `.tscn` scenes.

Also: `sandbox/` grey-box test scene, `dev/` dev-only helpers, `tests/` xUnit tests for pure logic, plus `tests/ContentTests`, which reads the content as text: conditions parse and name real items, every Yarn node a scene or script plays exists, every clue flag has a notebook entry and the reverse, and only known characters speak (Yarn reads `Text:` at the start of a line as a speaker, so keep colons out of narration).

State and rules: `core/state/` (GameState, SaveData), `conditions/` and `puzzles/rules/` are pure C# with no Godot types, so `tests/` can compile them directly. Keep Godot code out of those folders. Read and change state through `Game.State` (`core/Game.cs`); gates use condition strings such as `"guts >= 2 | item:flashlight"` (`conditions/ConditionParser.cs` has the grammar).

Conventions: snake_case folders and scene files, PascalCase C# files, namespace `Pegasus.<Module>`. Game state is plain C# data kept apart from nodes; scenes rebuild from it on load. Grey-box art and placeholder sound until real assets land.

## Commands

- Build: `dotnet build`
- Tests: `dotnet test`
- Run headless: `godot --headless --quit-after 120 [res://scene.tscn]` (main scene is `core/boot.tscn`: continues the save; add `-- --new-game` to start fresh)
- Screenshot: `[SHOT_SIZE=1600x700] scripts/shot.sh out.png [res://scene.tscn] [frames] [extra dev args, e.g. --hold=move_right@1-40 --tap=interact@60]` (see `dev/DevDriver.cs`; `--state=/abs/state.json` starts from a save-format fixture, and each shot prints the player position and state; frames keep counting while the tree is paused; `--open=res://puzzles/x/x.tscn` opens a puzzle on frame 5 (name a gameplay scene too; it exits 1 if the open is blocked) and `--click=X,Y@F` clicks at 1280x720 viewport coordinates; `--at=X,Z` puts the player there on frame 2 (same: exits 1 with no player))
- After adding new assets or editing `.yarn` files, import: `godot --headless --import` (runs use the compiled Yarn until then; on a fresh checkout run it twice, the first pass can fail on new files)
